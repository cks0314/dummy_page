Navigation Environment

Figure 1. Navigation environment considered in this work. (a) Bicycle-model robot with state and control inputs. (b) Relative robot-obstacle geometry.

We consider a bicycle-model robot navigating toward a goal in an environment populated with independently maneuvering unicycle obstacles. Unlike constant-velocity obstacles, these agents may continuously change their speed and heading, producing time-varying relative motion. The robot must generate collision-free trajectories while respecting nonholonomic dynamics and reasoning about nearby obstacles in real time.

Central Research Question

"Can safety guarantees be recovered when surrounding obstacles are allowed to maneuver within known physical limits?"

Proposed Algorithm: AR-DPCBF

Instead of assuming constant obstacle velocity, AR-DPCBF models obstacle maneuverability explicitly and analytically reserves the safety margin required under worst-case bounded maneuvers.

The fundamental limitation of conventional DPCBF is that it certifies safety only for the nominal obstacle motion model. Once an obstacle accelerates or turns, the true barrier dynamics deviate from the nominal prediction, leading to silent safety failures.

AR-DPCBF addresses this limitation by treating each obstacle as a bounded adversary whose acceleration and turning rate are constrained by physical limits. Rather than reacting post-maneuver, the controller explicitly accounts for the worst-case admissible maneuver during barrier construction.

Bounded Adversary

Obstacles are modeled with dynamic capabilities bounded by known physical limits instead of constant-velocity assumptions.

Analytical Margin

The barrier contracts analytically as maneuverability increases, reserving exact safety margins for worst-case maneuvers.

Provable Safety

Guarantees remain formally valid under any obstacle maneuver within physical capability limits.

Abstract

Adversarial-Robust Dynamic Parabolic Control Barrier Functions (AR-DPCBF) extend DPCBFs to dynamic environments with maneuvering obstacles. Unlike DPCBF, which assumes constant obstacle velocity, AR-DPCBF models obstacle maneuvers through a bounded-adversary framework and derives a geometry-preserving robust safety certificate with formal guarantees.
