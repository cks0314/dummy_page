AR-DPCBF guarantees collision-free navigation under bounded adversarial obstacle maneuvers while maintaining real-time computational performance.

Navigation Environment

Figure 1. Navigation environment considered in this work. (a) Bicycle-model robot with state and control inputs. (b) Relative robot-obstacle geometry used throughout the paper.

We consider a bicycle-model robot navigating toward a goal in an environment populated with independently maneuvering unicycle obstacles. Unlike constant-velocity obstacles, these agents may continuously change their speed and heading, producing time-varying relative motion with the robot. The robot must generate collision-free trajectories while respecting its nonholonomic dynamics and continuously reasoning about the relative position and velocity of nearby obstacles in real time.


The Problem

Existing Dynamic Parabolic Control Barrier Functions (DPCBFs) assume obstacle velocity remains constant over the prediction horizon. However, real obstacles may accelerate, decelerate, or change their heading, introducing an unmodeled maneuvering effect that silently invalidates the nominal safety certificate.

Failure of conventional DPCBF. The controller continues to satisfy the nominal barrier constraint, yet the obstacle begins maneuvering, causing the underlying safety guarantee to become invalid.

Why does the safety certificate fail?

DPCBF assumes

$$\dot h = \dot h_{\mathrm{DPCBF}}$$

but the true barrier dynamics become

$$\dot h_{\mathrm{true}} = \dot h_{\mathrm{DPCBF}} + {\color{#d62728}{\Delta(t)}}$$

where the additional maneuvering term is

$$ \Delta(t)= a_{\rm obs}\cos\tilde{\theta}_{\rm obs} + v_{\rm obs}\omega_{\rm obs} \left( 2\lambda \dot{\tilde y}_{\rm rel} \cos\tilde{\theta}_{\rm obs} - \sin\tilde{\theta}_{\rm obs} \right). $$
