AR-DPCBF guarantees collision-free navigation under bounded adversarial obstacle maneuvers while maintaining real-time computational performance.

Navigation Environment

Figure 1. Navigation environment considered in this work. (a) Bicycle-model robot with state and control inputs. (b) Relative robot-obstacle geometry used throughout the paper.

We consider a bicycle-model mobile robot navigating toward a goal in dynamic environments populated with independently moving obstacles. The robot must generate collision-free trajectories while satisfying its nonholonomic motion constraints and reacting online to obstacle motion.


Unlike static environments, each obstacle possesses its own motion, characterized by its position, velocity, and potentially changing heading. As the robot and obstacles move simultaneously, collision avoidance depends not only on their instantaneous separation but also on their relative motion. Consequently, both the relative position and relative velocity between the robot and nearby obstacles must be considered when reasoning about safety.


Throughout this work, the robot interacts with multiple maneuvering obstacles whose future motion cannot be assumed to remain perfectly constant. The objective is to safely navigate to the desired goal while continuously adapting to changes in obstacle motion in real time.
